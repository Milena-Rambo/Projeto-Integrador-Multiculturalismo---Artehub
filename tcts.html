Cross-Cutting Contemporary Themes (TCTs)

Themes that are part of our life in society and can be studied in several school subjects at the same time.

What are the TCTs?

The Cross-Cutting Contemporary Themes, or TCTs (“Temas Contemporâneos Transversais” in Portuguese), are subjects that matter for life in society, such as respect, citizenship, the environment, technology and health.

They are called cross-cutting because they do not belong to a single subject. They can be worked on in Arts, Portuguese, History, Geography, Science and many other classes, helping connect what we learn at school with what we live every day.

In Brazil’s National Common Curricular Base (BNCC), they are organized into six major areas.

The six TCT areas

  • Citizenship and Civic Life
  • Science and Technology
  • Economy
  • Environment
  • Multiculturalism
  • Health

TCTs related to ArteHub

Not all themes are connected to our project in the same way. The ones with the “direct relation” label are the closest to the Art without Borders proposal. The others relate to it in a complementary way.

Direct relation

Multiculturalism

It is the coexistence of different cultures, traditions, identities, languages and customs in the same society. It is the central theme of ArteHub.

Practical example: learning about the German tradition of Alto Santa Fé and the Spanish phrase in the chronicle.
Direct relation

Cultural diversity

Recognizing and valuing the cultural differences that exist in society, understanding that they enrich how we live together.

Practical example: realizing that a person’s accent is part of their identity and should not be a reason for jokes.
Direct relation

Human Rights Education

It is linked to respect, equality, human dignity and the fight against prejudice and discrimination.

Practical example: defending that no one should be made fun of for their origin, appearance or way of speaking.
Direct relation

Citizenship and Civic Life

It involves respectful coexistence, social participation and each person’s responsibility toward the group.

Practical example: talking at school about how to treat classmates with respect and how to build better coexistence.
Complementary relation

Science and Technology

Digital media make it possible to share artistic productions, stories and cultural knowledge with many people.

Practical example: publishing this website, with texts, images and videos, so anyone can access it.
Complementary relation

Environment

Peoples relate to nature in different ways and value their territories in their own ways.

Practical example: observing how life in the countryside and agriculture are part of the culture of Alto Santa Fé.
In short: Multiculturalism, Cultural diversity, Human Rights Education and Citizenship and Civic Life are the closest to the project. Science and Technology and Environment appear as support, not as the main focus.

How ArteHub relates to the TCTs

ArteHub uses art to talk about multiculturalism. By presenting the cultures of the places where we live, we work on cultural diversity. By defending respect for differences, we connect with human rights and citizenship.

Technology helps us share all of this on a website in three languages, and our connection with the places where we live shows a little of how each community relates to its territory.

In this way, the project shows that one theme can be studied in several subjects at the same time, which is exactly the idea behind the TCTs.
