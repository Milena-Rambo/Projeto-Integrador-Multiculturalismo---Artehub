Our proposal: Art without Borders

Presentation of the proposal

ArteHub presents the proposal “Art without Borders,” an initiative that uses different forms of artistic expression to show that cultural diversity is present in our daily lives and is part of the identity of each person and community.

The idea came from realizing that we often live alongside people who have stories, customs, accents, traditions and experiences different from ours, but we do not always pay attention to the value of those differences.

Art can bring these realities closer and turn individual experiences into opportunities for learning.

Through paintings, photographs, music, dance, literature, videos and digital productions, we want to present different cultural expressions and encourage coexistence based on respect, curiosity and appreciation of identities.

Justification

Multiculturalism is related to the coexistence of different cultures in the same society.

It appears in languages, religions, traditions, food, celebrations, customs and also in the different ways of thinking and expressing ourselves.

However, differences are not always understood or respected. An accent, a family tradition, an appearance or a different custom can end up being the reason for jokes, judgments and prejudice.

For this reason, we believe it is important to develop a proposal that encourages people to get to know other realities and to realize that no culture should be considered superior to another.

Art was chosen as the main path because it allows us to share feelings, stories and knowledge in different ways.

General objective

To promote the appreciation of multiculturalism through art, encouraging respect for differences, recognition of cultural identities and dialogue between people from different origins and realities.

Specific objectives

  • To recognize different cultural expressions present in Brazil and in the world.
  • To value the diversity of accents, languages, traditions and customs.
  • To use art as a tool for expression and awareness.
  • To fight stereotypes, prejudice and judgments related to cultural differences.
  • To learn about the cultural characteristics of the municipalities and communities where we live.
  • To encourage the exchange of experiences and dialogue between different people.
  • To show that diversity contributes to building the identity of a society.

How would the proposal be carried out?

We thought of five actions that complement each other:

1. Multicultural gallery

Gather photographs, paintings, drawings and other works that represent different cultures, traditions and ways of life, organized in a gallery for everyone to explore.

2. Voices and stories

Record accounts, accents, expressions and memories of people from different backgrounds, in texts, videos or other forms, so that every voice is heard with respect.

3. Map of cultures

Show the places where we live and the cultural characteristics of each one, such as celebrations, traditions and customs, to see how realities are similar and also different.

4. Art that communicates

Create artistic works, such as posters, drawings, photographs and videos, with messages of respect and against prejudice.

5. Digital exhibition

Bring everything together in an online space, like this website, so that students, teachers and families can visit, learn and share.

Target audience

The proposal is aimed mainly at students, teachers and the school community, but it can also reach families and other people interested in learning about and valuing cultural diversity.

Expected results

We hope the initiative helps broaden knowledge about different cultures and encourages more respectful attitudes in everyday life.

We also aim to stimulate creativity, student participation and the recognition of art as an important form of communication.

ArteHub’s message

We believe diversity should not be a reason for separation, but an opportunity for learning.

Each culture has its own particularities, memories and ways of expression.

Art does not need to speak the same language to share a message. It can bring people closer, cross borders and show that our differences are also what connects us.

Our relationship with multiculturalism

Much More Than an Accent

There are things we only notice after spending a lot of time with someone. At my school, I had two friends who spoke differently from the rest of the class. One of them was from Rio Grande do Sul, in southern Brazil, and had such a strong, drawn-out “R” that whenever she said certain words, someone always showed up to imitate her.

Sometimes, the person did not even wait for her to finish talking. All it took was one word with that accent and someone would repeat everything, exaggerating the “R” on purpose. The others would start laughing, and soon the conversation went on as if nothing had happened. My friend usually laughed too. I think that is why nobody stopped to think much about it.

I was also used to the way she talked. After a while, it did not even seem that different anymore. The funny thing is that, when someone imitated her voice, the accent seemed much more exaggerated than it really was. It was as if the person took one single trait of hers and turned it into everything she was.

My other friend had Spanish roots and could speak a little of the language. Every now and then, she repeated a phrase her grandmother used to say, “¡Sana, sana, culito de rana, si no sana hoy, sanará mañana!”. I did not really know what it meant, but I was curious whenever I heard those words. She said the phrase naturally, like someone repeating something she had heard at home so many times that she knew the sound of every word by heart. It was a small piece of her family’s story that, every now and then, showed up in our conversations at school.

I had never stopped to think that these two situations were so different. When my friend from Rio Grande do Sul spoke, someone could turn her accent into a joke. But when my other friend spoke Spanish, I wanted to know what those words meant and where they came from. One thing seemed funny to others, the other sparked curiosity. And I was also part of that group that listened, laughed or got curious, without thinking much about why.

What bothers me a little when I remember this is that my friend from Rio Grande do Sul did not even have to say anything funny. Sometimes, just speaking was enough for someone to imitate the way she talked. Since she laughed along, it seemed like everything was fine. But did she find it funny every single time? Maybe she really did not mind, maybe some jokes were funnier than others. The fact is that, to us, that “R” stood out; to her, it was simply the way she spoke.

Besides, I never heard anyone say that we spoke with an accent. We just spoke, and that was it. The accent seemed to exist only in her voice, as if our way of pronouncing words was the normal one and hers was a different version. If someone from somewhere else came to our school, they might also find our way of speaking strange.

The Spanish phrase from my other friend also made me think about the story behind those words. I did not know her grandmother, nor did I know when she used to say it. I only knew the phrase that had been passed from one generation to another and now showed up in the middle of our conversations. It was curious to think that something so simple could have so much history behind it.

Today, when I remember these situations, I do not think about big events. I remember the “R” that someone always ended up imitating and the Spanish phrase my friend always said. Back then, they were just details of our everyday life, things that happened at school and soon gave way to other topics.

But maybe that is exactly what makes these memories interesting. We spend time with people, listen to their stories, get used to their quirks, and do not always notice what is behind them. And sometimes, only after a while do we start paying attention to what had always been there.
